The Brief

How do you make a technical product feel like a design choice?

Blindex makes custom roller blinds for offices, hotels, and commercial spaces. The client came to me needing a full brand identity — logo, guidelines, the works.
The real challenge wasn't the aesthetics. It was figuring out how to communicate something functional and technical in a way that felt refined and considered — without losing sight of what the product actually does.

The brief said "elegant and professional." I kept asking: what does elegant actually look like for a blind that disappears into a ceiling?

The Idea

Starting from the product, not the mood board

I built the concept around what the product actually does: it moves. Smoothly, quietly, always controlled. That gesture — a roller blind in motion — became the foundation for everything: the logo, the tone, the spacing, the palette.
I called it Dynamic Flow. Not as a tagline, but as a design principle to keep every decision anchored to something real.

Visual Identity

The Mark

One letter does all the work

I wanted a wordmark with a handcrafted feel — soft curves, continuous stroke, nothing rigid. The letterforms needed to communicate quality without looking like every other sans-serif identity out there.
The "e" became the key. Its shape naturally echoes the scrolling motion of a blind — fluid, controlled, never breaking. Giving it a different colour wasn't just a stylistic choice: it's where the whole concept lives, visible at any size.

Color Palette

Deep Black and Ardesia Red anchor the primary system. The secondaries — Smoke, Pearl, Sage, Sand — give the brand room to breathe across different contexts without losing coherence. Sand is the default background: warm, quiet, never competing with the content.
